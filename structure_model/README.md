## Findings

### Wire: ~1 GPa working stress is reachable, at the low end of bridge safety factors

- **Strength.** Suspension-bridge wire (~5 mm) comes in guaranteed classes of 1,570, 1,770 and 1,960 MPa, with a 0.2% proof stress of at least 75% of the guaranteed strength [Tokyo Rope]. Akashi Kaikyō used 1,765 MPa wire (180 kgf/mm², up from the usual 160) [JSCE 1995; FHWA]. Thinner wire is stronger: tire cord reaches ~3.2–3.5 GPa and saw wire ~4 GPa [encyclopedia.pub; Kobelco], and Bekaert's empirical high-strength threshold rises from ~1,460 MPa at 5 mm to ~2,250 MPa at 1 mm [US 4,854,032].
- **Safety factor.** Existing US bridge cables call for action below a factor of 2.15 (NCHRP Report 534, as applied in an Ohio DOT evaluation that found 2.41–2.44) [Ohio DOT 2013]. A Japanese study found cables designed with a factor of 2.0 on live loads still resist large earthquakes, and another proposes a cable factor of 1.8 in a load-factor format [JSCE 1996, 2002]. The design factors of ~2.5 for new cables and ~2.2 for Akashi-class spans are commonly quoted but weren't verified here: NCHRP 534 itself (onlinepubs.trb.org) couldn't be reached from this session.
- **So:** 1 GPa is a factor of ~1.96 on the strongest bridge class, at the low end of practice; or a factor of ~2.25 on ~1 mm wire. The spin load is static and well known, and the spokes are in vacuum (no corrosion, no hydrogen embrittlement), which argue for a lower factor than bridges, which carry traffic and weather. **The design need not change**, but the factor of safety is now a design decision to record: ~1 GPa means SF ~2 on bridge wire, or thinner wire.
- **Range in the system model** narrowed from 700–1,200 to **800–1,200 MPa**: 1,770 MPa wire at SF 2.2 to ~1 mm wire at SF ~1.9.

### Wire chemistry: the carbonyl route matters

- High-carbon wire steel tolerates very little phosphorus, sulfur or nickel: tire-cord patents limit P to ≤ 0.02%, S to ≤ 0.015% (preferably < 0.008%) and Ni to ≤ 0.05%; phosphorus embrittles the grain boundaries and sulfur forms MnS inclusions that break wires during drawing [wire-rod and metal-cord patents; Kobe Steel study; limits from search summaries]. Small Ni additions (0.05–0.8%) can help drawability, but ≥ 0.05% Ni has been reported to suppress the pearlite transformation in patenting.
- Chondritic metal is far outside that: with the notes' H-chondrite values (~1.6% Ni in the rock, ~17% free metal), the metal holds ~9% Ni, more in L and LL metal, plus phosphorus (phosphides) and sulfur (troilite) nearby.
- **So the spokes depend on refining**, not on the metal grains as mined. The carbonyl process the notes propose (§5a, §14) does it: nickel leaves at low pressure, and carbonyl iron is very pure (reduced grades > 99.5% Fe, < 0.1% C, < 0.01% N, < 0.5% O), because the pentacarbonyl itself is distilled clean [carbonyl-iron patents]. Pearlitic wire is then carbonyl iron + ~0.8% C, with a little Mn and Si for patenting, like ordinary bridge wire. No literature on drawing wire from meteoritic Fe-Ni directly was found; the Earth test (plan §8) should use carbonyl-refined iron and, for comparison, the as-separated metal.

### Creep: plan for ~0.5–1.3 m of stretch

Cold-drawn wire relaxes ~5% in 1,000 h at half its strength (r = 30 f/f_u − 10%), and about twice that in the long term [BME Periodica Polytechnica]. Read as creep under constant load, a spoke at 1 GPa on 1,960 MPa wire stretches by ~0.2 m (400 m torus), ~0.5 m (900 m), ~0.85 m (1.6 km) and ~1.3 m (2.5 km) over the long term. The "compliant joints" of the notes therefore need adjustable lengths of order a metre, or the chords must be pre-stretched.

### Tangent-chord net: same mass as radial spokes; taper barely helps

- A chord at angle θ to the radial (sin θ = c/r) pulls the rim at only cos θ, but its length per anchor is r cos θ instead of r, so the mass is the same as radial spokes: **within 0.06%** at every size, not the ~0.2% loss the notes estimate. The system model's spoke formula therefore holds for the net.
- With 1.1 × the hub radius as the tangent circle, θ ≈ 4.3° at every size (the geometry is self-similar), as in the notes.
- **Tapering** the chords to uniform stress saves only 1.2% at 900 m, 2.2% at 1.6 km and 3.6% at 2.5 km; uniform cables are fine.
- **Bowing.** The across-chord component of the spin load (ω² c) bows each chord away from the axis by ρω²cL²/2σ: ~2.3 m at 900 m, ~7 m at 1.6 km, ~18 m at 2.5 km. The window for the hub and factory grows by that much; the chords cross at their bowed positions.
- At one anchor every 10 m, each chord end carries ~105 MN at 900 m and ~180 MN at 1.6 km. A **uniform** chord needs ~1,080 cm² at 900 m (a cable ~37 cm across) and ~1,920 cm² at 1.6 km, sized at mid-chord where its own spin load adds most; ~1,050 / ~1,800 cm² is only the end area of a tapered chord.

### Spin-up of a partial ring (provisional)

- A chord's far end lands 2θ r off the point opposite (134 m at 900 m, 238 m at 1.6 km). Two opposite arcs therefore each have a **single-laced zone of 2θ r at each end**, where anchors keep only the chord whose far end lands on the other arc. Every anchor has a chord only if each arc spans more than 4θ r: ~270 m at 900 m (about five 56 m segments, ~0.56 Mt with rock), ~480 m at 1.6 km.
- **Assembled statics** (`net_statics.py`): each chord is one elastic member with a single tension, the rim is cut into rigid segments, and the joints are springs, *sliding* (free along the rim, shear only), *pinned* (tension and shear) or *locked* (also bending). Chords are tension-only, with the taut set iterated both ways until it is exactly the set of stretched chords. A stage counts as resolved only if that set converges, each segment's force and moment balance holds (residuals ~1e-5 or better), and, once the assembly's rigid motion is removed, segment rotations stay below 0.05 rad and displacements below 1% of the radius at the spin rate reported. Otherwise it is left unresolved, for a geometrically nonlinear analysis. It reproduces the finished net: with sliding joints, chord tensions within ±4% of the formula (the spread is the discrete anchor positions). A first, local estimate (`spinup.partial_arc`) assigned tensions anchor by anchor, so one cable could carry different tensions at its two ends; it is kept only as a cross-check of the joint tension.
- **The chords carry twice their finished load on the shortest arcs.** With locked joints every chord of a ~4θ r arc pulls ~2× its finished tension: ~2,000 MPa at full spin in cables sized for 1 GPa. Stresses scale with spin², so **this stage must stay below ~0.71 of the final spin rate** (at every size), or use extra cables, or hang longer arcs (0.83 at ~1.5 × 4θ r, 0.92 at ~3 ×, at 900 m).
- **The joints carry tension and bending until the ring closes.** ~0.9–1% of a full ring's hoop force on the shortest arcs (~176 MN at 900 m at full spin, ~550 MN at 1.6 km), close to the local estimate's 1.1%, plus a moment (~6 GN·m at 900 m). Longer locked arcs carry less chord load but much larger moments (~70 GN·m at 1.5 × 4θ r, ~220 GN·m at 3 × at 900 m): with rigid segments that depends heavily on the model, and it is the case for the finite-element model.
- **Sliding joints can't hold a partial ring** (no equilibrium: the end segments have nothing to resist their pull along the rim), and pinned joints give no resolved partial stage either: a mechanism, or segment rotations of 0.5–1.9 rad, far outside the linear model. So the joints must be locked during construction; every locked stage checked is resolved, with rotations below 0.003 rad.
- **And released once the ring is closed:** with locked joints the closed ring behaves as a hoop and the hull takes ~64% of the load at 900 m (~50% at 1.6 km) in this model, as the notes warn (strain compatibility). The joints must slide along the rim after closure, or the spokes be made stiffer.
- **Imbalance:** the stack spins freely about its centre of mass, so a mismatch between paired arcs shifts the spin axis away from the assembly's geometric centre rather than loading a bearing: ~3.6 m for a 1% mismatch on the first pair of 900 m arcs, ~7.5 m at 2.5 km. The hub and the net move together, so this is not a loss of clearance between them; their relative motion needs the stabilising lines' stiffness, still open.

- **Baseline: built at rest** (`rest.py`). A child's stack is assembled at rest and spun up finished, so none of the partial-ring stages below arise; they are the option of adding a torus to a living stack, at full spin, and check selected stages of it. For the baseline, in the ring's plane, with the nonlinear model below:
  - **At rest** the chords are pretensioned (a strain of 5 × 10⁻⁴ as installed, to stay taut through a ±20 K swing against the hull, a guess) and only the hull resists them: the closed ring is squeezed, its joints bear on stops, and the hull carries ~74 MPa of compression at 900 m (~81 at 1.6 km; ~110–120 with the chords 20 K colder, against an allowable ~150), the chords keeping ~2–3% of their finished tension. The chords hold the ring far from buckling in its plane (~50× the pretension at 900 m). The hub is held in its window by that pretension alone: ~1.8 MN/m at 900 m, a docking ship moving it ~5 cm.
  - **Spinning up**, the hoop's compression falls linearly with the spin load and is gone at ~0.32 of the spin; the joints open together and the chords carry the rim alone, as finished. Every chord's tension on the way is at most its own at full spin, as drawn and with the chords' lengths (±2 cm) and the rim's mass per anchor (±2%) scattered as built; the hull's worst is the at-rest compression, its bending a few MPa. Nothing exceeds the finished state but that pretension.
  - **The joints need a spring along the rim:** with joints that only slide, the near-diametral chords barely hold the segments along the rim (odd harmonics hardly stretch them), and a 2% mass scatter slides segments by metres; ~10⁸ N/m per joint holds them for a few MPa in the hull. Each joint opens ~0.15–0.4 m by full spin. Finished, the scattered net's chords spread by ±~7%: the rim's mass must be placed within ~2% per anchor, or the chords trimmed.
  - **The spin-up's torque is negligible:** even over 30 days its loads are millionths of the spin's; the farm rings follow on their radial spokes alone, lagging ~0.25–0.8 m at rest, the laced lines and the lattice carry it with ample margin. Energy sets the time: water thrusters take ~4–5 years of a torus's own power, host rock in a mass driver at ~0.5 km/s ~5–7 months (proposed).
  - **The fill must be compacted as placed** (proposed): loose crushed rock (~1.6–1.9 t/m³ against 2.0 finished) settles 5–20%, and at the tube's sides that opens a slot 0.5–2 m high at the top of every cell, through the jacket's whole thickness, adding tens of mSv/yr where it faces; compacted, the slots are 1–5 cm (under ~2 mSv/yr), or the side cells are topped up after spin-up.
  - Not modelled: out of the ring's plane, the stops and springs as hardware, the fill's flow in its cells, the stack's attitude at rest.
- **Geometrically nonlinear** (`nonlinear.py`, `sequence.py`): the rim as corotational beams with the hull and frames' E A and E I (a thin tube of the tube's radius), the joints as penalty springs, each chord a tension-only cable with its geometric stiffness and a slight pretension, the rim's and chords' centrifugal loads at their current positions with their load stiffness, the centre of mass and mean rotation held by multipliers, the spin stepped up with Newton iterations and line search, joints released step by step from a locked equilibrium. Checks: a cantilever and a rigid rotation of one element; it reproduces the rigid model on the shortest locked arcs (chords ~1.94×, joints ~1% of the hoop) and the finished net (within 1%).
  - Pinned or sliding joints lose equilibrium when released from a locked state (the shortest arcs fold by ~40° with overloaded chords): joints stay locked.
  - With flexible segments longer arcs don't relieve the chords (~1.8×, spin limit ~0.73–0.80 against the rigid model's 0.83–0.92) and bend the hull to ~600–1,000 MPa at full spin.
  - Temporary radial lines from the hub to the single-laced anchors hold every stage at full spin. Every cable is checked at its peak, its own spin load included (a radial line's, conservatively, all of it at its hub end): at a chord's area the lines are ~5% over, so 1.15× with a 1.05× sleeve on the end cables (`sequence.SUPPORT`): lines ≤ ~0.99× their allowable, chords ~1% past it (as the finished net's own ~1.007×, from the discrete anchors), hull bending ≤ ~55 MPa, joints ~0.5–0.7% of the hoop; ~47 kt at 900 m, ~270 kt at 1.6 km; the hub carries ~3.0 / ~9.5 GN across. Doubling the end-zone cables relieves the chords but not the hull.
  - Closed with every joint locked, the hull carries ~700 MPa as a hoop at full spin; closed on sliding closure joints, ~2 MPa, and releasing every joint gives the finished net, the chords ~7% past their allowable at worst on the way (~0.97 of the spin for that step).

### Tube cross-section: anchor the chords at the tube's sides

- **Model** (`tube_section.py`): one metre of tube in the plane of its cross-section. The pressure hull and the outer cell wall are membrane skins (bars); the jacket's transverse bulkheads, one every `cell_size_m` (10 m), are smeared into a plane-stress annulus of Q4 elements between them. Loads: spin gravity w²x on the rock (resting on the outer wall on the floor side, on the hull on the hub-facing side), on the fill, lakes and air (on the floor half of the hull, by projected width), on the bumper and on the steel; 70 kPa on the hull. Each anchor is a fitting through the jacket's depth, spreading the chord's pull over the radial line of nodes at its angle (a single node would be a point load, whose stresses grow without bound as the plate around it thickens). It is pinned at the chord's attachment on the outer wall: the line's x displacements follow a free rotation about that point (Lagrange multipliers), so the fitting passes on a force but no couple. A chord sized at the working stress stretches by σL/E whatever its load, so all anchors move together and fixed supports are exact; anchors that would push are released. Every part is sized fully stressed at `plate_stress_mpa` (300 MPa, a guess) in von Mises at every integration point, or held at its minimum gauge (`skin_min_gauge_m` 1 cm, `cell_wall_min_gauge_m` 5 mm). Compression is allowed half the working stress, blended with tension by the compression's share of the stress state (pure shear: three quarters), an allowance for stiffeners, not a buckling check; a step at the tension/compression switch kept the sizing from settling. The sizing runs until every part is within 2% of its allowable or at its minimum gauge; a result that doesn't get there is flagged (`converged`, `sized`). The skins also carry the hull's lengthwise pressure load (pa/2) and the pillowing of their panels between bulkheads; hub-facing hull panels pressed in by the rock get stiffeners (vaulted), or the rock rests on hung membrane cell floors (hung). Checks: a Q4 patch test, no support moment about each chord's attachment, the hoop force pa under pressure alone, the rock's weight entering only at the walls, reactions equal to the loads, the load equal to the system model's rim load (within 3%), convergence of the sizing at every size, and mesh convergence (0.4% from 4 × 144 to 8 × 288 elements).
- **Anchors at the sides.** At the tube's two sides (φ = 90° and 270°), the floor half hangs between them and the hub-facing half stands as an arch: hull and frames ~0.055 / 0.28 / 1.1 / 4.6 Mt at 400 m / 900 m / 1.6 km / 2.5 km, against the r^2.3 estimate's 0.046 / 0.30 / 1.1 / 3.1 Mt (+19% / −8% / +1% / +46%). With the spokes, the steel per torus moves by −2% to +7%, well inside the plan's ~30% threshold.
- **On the crest** (one net in the mid-plane) the section bends like a ring hung from one point: ~0.92 Mt at 900 m, ~8 Mt at 1.6 km, ~55 Mt at 2.5 km. Anchors spread over the hub-facing half settle on the two sides (~85–97% of the load) and save only ~2–6%.
- **So each torus has two nets, one in each side plane of its tube** (~34 m from the mid-plane at 900 m), and the mid-plane is clear of chords between them.
- **Vaulted vs hung ceiling cells:** within ~7% in mass (hung heavier).
- **Sensitivity at 900 m:** the skins' minimum gauge dominates (2 cm: +56%; 5 mm: −19%); working stress 200 / 500 MPa: +20% / −10%; 1 cm bulkheads and cell walls: +13%.
- **Not modelled:** buckling of the hub-facing half (up to ~1.3 MN/m of compression in the skins at 900 m, ~10 MN/m at 2.5 km), the segment joints and seals, the anchors' local details, and the valley's buildings, light box and ceiling.

### The hub in the nets: stabilising lines

- **Model** (`hub.py`): one torus and its share of the hub (the system model's hub, dock and factory metal, 25% of the torus steel). In each of the two nets the chords pass the hub at 1.1 × its radius, bowed outward, and a line runs from the hub to every chord's tangent point. A chord pulled sideways at its middle resists like a taut string, k = 2Tβ cot(βL) with β² = μ_c ω²/T for its own spin load (~2T/L); a line and its chord act in series; N lines spread around the window give Nk/2 in any direction of a net's plane. A torus and its hub turn about their common centre of mass: statics of two bodies joined by a spring in the rotating frame, and their relative motion with the reduced mass μ, including Coriolis (`modes`: two modes at |√(K/μ) ∓ ω| when K is the same in every direction; growth when it isn't and ω falls between). Lines are preloaded 1.5× their largest swing of tension, in service and while building; area, preload, stiffness and load cases are iterated until they agree, every line taut and within the wire stress.
- **Finished torus:** lateral stiffness ~31 / 65 / 114 / 179 MN/m at 400 m / 900 m / 1.6 km / 2.5 km, ~39 / 21 / 13 / 9 times μω²; natural frequency ~6.3 / 4.5 / 3.6 / 2.9 times the spin (inertial), split by Coriolis into ~5.3 and 7.3 / 3.5 and 5.5 / 2.6 and 4.6 / 1.9 and 3.9 times the spin in the rotating frame. 1% of the hub's mass at its rim, a 1% mismatch between opposite arcs or the orbit-raising thrust move the hub by up to ~3 cm at 900 m (~22 cm at 2.5 km). Lines ~170 mm² at 900 m; ~0.5 / 13 / 140 / 1,100 t per torus.
- **Building:** a single opposite pair of arcs holds the hub along its chords only by preload: without it the hub's motion grows (an asymmetric rotor, ×e every ~0.2 turns); with ~0.29 MN per line at 900 m it is held, but its chords are pulled ~2.4 m into an ~8 m window and the hub swings ~9 m off the arcs. Two pairs at right angles hold it (margin 7.9 / 4.4 / 2.9 / 2.1 without preload), preloaded ~25 / 100 / 300 / 750 kN per line to stay taut under a 1% mismatch, with the hub ~0.1 / 0.6 / 1.7 / 4.2 m off the arcs.
- **Axial and torsion:** radial lines give only their preload's geometric stiffness: the axial natural frequency is ~0.5 of the spin. Inclining 10% of the lines from the hub's mid-plane to the side nets (their full length, e.g. ~35 m at 900 m) gives ~8–25× more axial stiffness, above the spin rate, for ~0.25 / 4 / 32 / 170 t more line.
- **Not modelled:** the hub as a beam between tori, the elevator, pipe and power shafts, docking loads, torque through the lines.
